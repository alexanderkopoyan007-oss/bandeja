# Shots, movement and feel: published numbers for BANDEJA

Research notes, 2026-10-27. Every figure has its source and how it was measured. "Tennis analogue" marks a
tennis (or other sport) number used because padel has none. "Derived" marks arithmetic done here on published
numbers. Anything not published is said plainly. Court facts used below: 20 × 10 m, net 0.88 m at the centre,
service line 6.95 m from the net (FIP *Rules of Padel*, review applying from 1 Jan 2026,
[padelfip.com PDF](https://www.padelfip.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/FIP_Rules-of-Padel.pdf)).

Abbreviations: W = winner, FE = forced error, UE = unforced error, cont = ball kept in play.

**What the game should use.** Test the AI over many points against: men 10–11 shots and about 14 s a point, about
0.8 shots/s; rally-length shares near 26/26/22/26% for 1–4/5–7/8–11/>11 s; point endings about 35–42% winners,
25–33% forced, 29–38% unforced (main-draw men); about half of errors into the net. Per-stroke targets from §1a:
smash about 45% winners and 6% UE; bandeja about 3–4% winners and 3% UE (a keep-the-net shot, not a finisher);
volleys 3–6% winners, 3–5% UE; groundstrokes and back-wall exits under 1% winners, 5–6% UE; first-serve faults
about 10%, double faults about 2% of second serves. Flat smash about 50% winners for men, 33% for women.

**What the game should use.** Smash ball speed: about 125–135 km/h unopposed and 105–120 km/h in play (the
current 138 km/h median sits at the top of the band; check it against the opposed figure). Serve about 60–70 km/h
for a strong club player; no pro serve speed is published. Swing timing: forward swing about 0.26 s (bandeja),
0.37–0.39 s (back-wall exit, por tres smash), about 0.32 s for a full drive (tennis); preparation about 0.5 s. Volley
racket speed should sit about 5–10% under the groundstroke at the same effort, with a shorter preparation (0.11 vs
0.15 s). Spread of landing points: a 2D normal with the long axis along the shot line; mean miss in depth about
1.2 m (forehand) and 1.6 m (backhand) for advanced tennis players, forehand tighter than backhand. Keep contact time at about 4–5 ms
until a padel figure exists.

**What the game should use.** Model contact error in space (cm along the ball's path), not time: the time window
is the usable hitting length divided by the ball–racket relative speed (derived: a 10 cm zone at 25 m/s is 4 ms,
at 15 m/s 6.7 ms), which reproduces the finding that faster balls give smaller timing errors for the same skill.
Equivalent timing SD: about 5–7 ms for an elite AI, 10–15 ms for a good club AI, 20+ ms for a beginner (the last
two are scaled from the lab range 12–23 ms, not measured in padel). Visual reaction delay before a player can
commit to a direction: 140–190 ms after the opponent's contact (Triolet; Mecheri racket start); feet start about
270–310 ms after contact; mid-flight corrections need 110–170 ms. Time available: about 0.2–0.3 s for net
exchanges, about 0.9 s from return to third shot, 1.25 s average between shots.

| Split-step height; padel split-step timing; how players turn vs shuffle when retreating to the back glass; lunge data | — | **not found** (only retailer coaching blogs, e.g. TiendaPadelPoint, which advise side steps over cross-over steps near the wall and a set position before the bounce) | — |

**What the game should use.** AI split-step: take off at about the opponent's contact (0 to +40 ms), land
140–160 ms after it, airborne about 120–150 ms; then pick the direction. Landing earlier than about
(reaction time − 1 SD) should allow a faster but sometimes wrong first step (Uzu). Without a split-step the first
step should cost about 100 ms more to reach the same point (Uzu 764 vs 868 ms). In padel most moves are short and
sideways or forwards; backward runs are rare and should mostly be shuffles or a turn-and-run before playing off
the glass, with a two-step adjustment (as in Espino's patterns) into the stroke.

**What the game should use.** Attack position about 3.5–4.5 m from the net (pros nearer 3.8 m, club players
4.5 m). Defenders after a deep lob: pushed back to 5.5 m, or to about 8.5 m (near the glass) after an excellent
lob. Partners shift together toward the ball's side; in depth, keep a stagger when one closes on the net.
Lobs should be about 60% of the ways a defending pair regains the net. The serving pair should win about 98% of
1–2-shot rallies, about 64% of 3–6-shot ones, about 57% of 7–8, and no more than half of longer ones; overall
about 62% of service points for men and about 49% for women. Level: an amateur AI should raise UEs (to about 37%+
of endings) rather than lose winners.

**What the game should use.** Input to photon at or under about 50–67 ms (3–4 frames at 60 Hz) in the browser;
treat 100 ms as the point where control feels laggy. Use a graded swing-timing window rather than a pass/fail one:
a perfect band about ±20–30 ms, good to about ±60–90 ms, playable to about ±135 ms, with the quality of contact
falling smoothly through the racket model. That is 3–5× wider than real elite precision (5–7 ms SD), which a
player cannot reach through a keyboard and a 60 Hz screen; the AI's own timing errors should use §3's numbers.
